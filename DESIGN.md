# Post Rider: Design

Minecraft 1.21.1, NeoForge 21.1.x. Standalone (no other mods required). All items are server-side vanilla-style items.

## Workflow rules
- Every model is built in Blockbench first (never hand-written JSON imported later) and approved before work continues.
- Every feature ships with unit tests (`src/test/java`, NeoForge `unitTest`).
- Anything uncertain gets asked, not assumed.

## Mailbox
- Style: US post box on a wooden post, door at the front, side flag that goes up when the inbox has mail. Two blocks tall. Only the box takes the dye colour.
- The flag is red on every colour except the red mailbox, which gets a yellow flag so it stands out.
- All 16 dye colours. Recipe: iron ingots around a chest, with a fence underneath (comes out white). Craft with a dye to recolour, or right-click a placed mailbox with a dye.
- Placement is always allowed. If it has no sky view, the placer gets a warning that the postman will probably lose mail.
- Owner types a name in the mailbox screen. Names are 3–24 characters and unique server-wide, ignoring case. A player can own several mailboxes.
- Only the owner can open it, and only the owner (or an operator) can break it.
- Inbox: 27 sealed parcels/letters. When broken, the contents spill on the ground and the name is freed.
- Sending: the mailbox screen has a 9-slot parcel area, an address picked from a list of all mailboxes, and a Send button. Postage is free.

## Parcel and letters
- Parcel item: sealed, holds up to 9 stacks. Right-click to unpack into your inventory (overflow drops at your feet).
- Letters: craft a blank Letter, use it to open its own writing screen, and finish it to get a Written Letter. Using a Written Letter opens it to read. It stays a readable item you can keep.
- Put a Written Letter in the parcel slots to send it. If the parcel holds nothing but one letter, it travels and arrives as just the letter. If there's anything else in it, everything (letter included) arrives inside a Parcel.
- Every screen shares the old-time post office look: walnut panelling, a parchment work surface, brass fittings, postage-stamp tabs, red wax for the main action, and ledger pages for lists (`client/gui/PostOfficeStyle`, `textures/gui/post_office.png`).
- Failure letter: a readable Letter item (book-style page) giving the reason (e.g. "killed by a Zombie"), the Lost Parcel's X/Y/Z and the addressee.

## Postman
- The crew: twenty riders, each always the same person with their own face, name and horse (`Roster`), so you get to know them ("yay, Chris is here"). Only one of each is out at a time, which is also the 20-rider limit. Chris (just "Chris") rides Lightning, the fastest horse at 10.5 blocks/s; Gus Tanner's Molasses is the slowest at 7.0. Eight are women: Mattie, Rosa, Clara, Hattie, Lottie, Nell, Ada and Sadie. They ride on a slim build (7-wide head, 3-wide arms) with female-coded faces, and messages about them say "her".
- Each rider has a signature bandana and small outfit touches (vest and shirt colours, rolled sleeves, a patched vest, inky cuffs). Long hair (a braid, buns, pigtails, hair down the back) is drawn with extra head parts shown only for whoever wears it. Skins are generated by tools/rider_skins.js in Blockbench from the base skin.
- A custom player-like mob with an Old West post rider skin (wide-brimmed hat, bandana, leather vest), riding a vanilla-style horse.
- His horse wears a mochila: a leather cover over the saddle with four boxy mail pockets (two on each side). Each horse gets a random vanilla coat and markings, and a random speed that is never below 7 blocks/s.
- Pickup: he appears in a puff of smoke at the sender's mailbox, takes the parcel and summons his horse.
- Riders only collect in daytime: mail posted after dusk (6pm game time) waits in the queue until 6am, and the sender is told so.
- He appears and vanishes in a puff of smoke, and his horse arrives (when he whistles) and leaves the same way.
- Every rider and every horse gets a random Old West name.
- Delivery: he dismounts, puts the parcel in the inbox and vanishes in smoke with his horse.
- Same dimension only, no distance cap. He keeps a 7×7 area of chunks loaded around himself so he can plan a 48-block stretch ahead; only the 3×3 he's in the middle of ticks (the outer ring grows and spawns nothing). At most 20 riders out at once; extra mail waits its turn.
- Prefers land and takes a land detour of up to 2× the straight-line distance. Otherwise he crosses water on a ferry barge (flat barge with railings and a lantern post) that carries him and the horse. It's about 3 blocks wide by 5 long: he sits on a bench at the bow rowing with a pair of oars (the row animation), facing forward like a boat, and the horse stands on the deck behind him. The barge appears in a puff of smoke at the water's edge and vanishes in smoke on the far shore. Its lantern's flame is drawn full-bright so it glows at night (it doesn't light the surroundings).
- When to take the barge: he swerves round water (up to 80 degrees either way) while it pays. Once he has ridden more than twice the ground he's gained (plus 16 blocks of slack), or there's no dry way on at all, he launches the barge on open water ahead (needs room for its 3x5 hull) with his horse aboard. It rows at a boat's speed (8 blocks/s) towards the destination, keeps going day or night, and lands at the first dry ground by the bow: the horse appears on the bank, he remounts, and the barge vanishes. The barge can't be harmed.
- On the water he steers well clear of ocean monuments (24 blocks) and of any drowned he can see (12 blocks). Drowned (tridents included) and guardians still hunt him like a player, and pufferfish sting him, so a crossing can still go wrong.
- Takes any damage a player would: mobs, lightning, fall, fire, drowning, and players. Every hostile mob that hunts players targets him, and creepers explode on him.
- He carries two potions of Instant Health II a day (restocked at 6am). Whenever he drops below half health he drinks one on the spot (1.6 s, even mid-gallop), healing 4 hearts. They're for him only, not the horse, and any left when he dies break with him.
- If the horse dies, he carries on on foot (still using the barge on water).
- At dusk he looks up to 8 blocks around for a flat pitch big enough for the tent (pegs included) and a seat in front, with at least two clear blocks before any drop, and walks to it. With nowhere suitable he plays and sleeps in the open.
- Mannerisms on the road give him life. They only move his head, arms and hat, so they play on horseback or on foot:
  - Greetings: a player who comes within 12 blocks in sight gets a wave or a tip of the hat, once per encounter (it resets after 32+ blocks apart). Villagers within 8 blocks get a hat tip now and then (30%).
  - Every 20-45 seconds he does something that suits where he is: wipe his brow and swig from his canteen in hot biomes by day, shiver in the cold, pull his hat down when rain starts, yawn and stretch in the evening, scratch his head on a detour (and after being teleported free when stuck), shade his eyes or check his pocket watch on a long ride, swat flies in swamps and jungles, pat his horse and look back over his shoulder while riding, and anywhere check his satchel, adjust his hat, hum along, roll his neck or crack his knuckles. Never the same one twice running.
  - Once within 100 blocks of the destination he reads the letter's address.
  - Only while travelling: never while fleeing, camping, at a mailbox, or drinking.
- Nothing can shove him while he's planted on a job (camping, in the tent, or at a mailbox picking up, whistling or delivering); on the move he's jostled like anyone else.
- He keeps his eyes on his work: he never turns to watch players, and his head only follows where he's looking while he isn't busy with an animation.
- If anything attacks him while he's camping, he abandons camp (the tent packs away) and flees on foot from it and any hostile mob nearby until daylight, then whistles up his horse and rides on.
- Camps every night (dusk to dawn) in a tent entity model, with no blocks placed. At dusk he hammers in the pegs (holding a hammer, with a knock on each blow), the tent goes up behind him in a puff of smoke, and he sits playing the harmonica (CREDITS.md) until monsters come out (about 7:11pm), then turns in, lying on his back with his hat over his face and his boots sticking out of the door. While in the tent, mobs only notice him within 4 blocks. At 6am the tent packs away in a puff of smoke and he rides on. If night falls on water, he keeps going until he reaches land. If the night is skipped, he packs up in the morning.
- His horse gallops at full speed, and leaps gaps of up to 3 blocks onto ground at about the same height. He never aims for, jumps onto or teleports onto lava, fire, magma, cactus, berry bushes, powder snow, dripstone, cobwebs or water.
- Route: each 48-block stretch he picks the first dry way on (straight, then swerves up to 80 degrees) that vanilla pathfinding can actually reach, so he goes round ravines and cliffs; failing that, the path that gets him nearest.
- Unstuck: after 30 s with no progress, or 5 s without moving 2 blocks, he teleports himself and his horse/barge onto safe ground: 48 blocks ahead if he can, else a 24-block hop, else anywhere within 24 blocks (even back the way he came); or to the surface if he's underground. After 5 tries in a row with no progress, the delivery fails.

## Failure
- Fails when he dies or runs out of unstuck tries.
- Destination mailbox broken or renamed, or inbox full: the Lost Parcel is placed where the mailbox is or was.
- Lost Parcel: a placed block that never despawns. The sender, the recipient, the player who killed the postman, or an operator can open it or break it. Opening it puts its contents in your inventory and removes it. Anyone else who right-clicks it sees who sent it, where it was going and where it came from, but can't take it.
- Lost on the water (killed on the barge, say), it's a floating wreck instead: the soggy, slumped parcel lashed to planks from the barge with one of its oars floating alongside, waterlogged at the surface where he went down, with the skull in his hat or his map as on land. Taking it leaves water behind. Otherwise, with no ground nearby, it floats on any water close by.
- Its look: a battered, torn, grimy parcel with snapped string and a red LOST tag. If the rider was killed, a skull in his cowboy hat lies beside it. If he gave up after getting stuck, a crumpled map lies there instead.
- The sender gets a failure letter in their mailbox. If their mailbox is gone, the failure goes to chat only.

## Notices
- Recipient: chat message when mail arrives (if online), and the flag goes up.
- Sender: chat message when delivered, and a chat message when the postman fails (plus the letter).

## Build order
1. Mailbox: model → approval → block, colours, naming, screen, inbox, tests.
2. Parcel, letter, Lost Parcel (each model approved first).
3. Postman, horse, barge, tent (each model approved first), then travel, camping, unstuck and failure handling.
